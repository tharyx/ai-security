# STAR story worksheet

> Make 5 to 8 stories. Each should take about 90 seconds to tell. Use "I", not "we", for your own actions. Do not invent facts. Do not name your employer's confidential details (use "a financial client", "a production database").

**Story title (3 to 5 words):**
**Skills it shows (tick):** [ ] Pressure  [ ] Mistake and learning  [ ] Conflict  [ ] Leadership  [ ] Teamwork  [ ] Attention to detail  [ ] Automation  [ ] Communication  [ ] Prioritization  [ ] Learning fast

## S. Situation (2 sentences)

_Where, when (roughly), what was normal, what changed. Give only the context needed._

## T. Task (1 to 2 sentences)

_What was your job or goal? What was at stake (users, data, money, time)?_

## A. Action (4 to 6 steps, what YOU did)

1. _First I …_
2. _Then I …_
3. _I decided … because …_
4. _I communicated … to …_
5. _I checked … by …_

## R. Result (numbers + learning)

- **Result with a number:** _time saved, outage minutes avoided, % improvement, tickets reduced_
- **What changed for good:** _process, script, alert, policy_
- **What I learned / what I would do differently:** _one honest sentence_

## Translate it into security language

| My DBA words | Security words |
|---|---|
| | |

_Examples: audit trail = evidence / logs; listener log = network telemetry; RMAN restore = recovery; role cleanup = least privilege._

## Link to the job

_Which security situation does this story prove I can handle? (incident response, access review, backup recovery, change control, root cause analysis, working with developers)_

## Practice checklist

- [ ] Told it aloud in under 2 minutes
- [ ] Told it to someone who is not technical
- [ ] Has a number in the result
- [ ] Ends on the learning, not on the problem
- [ ] I can answer the follow-up "what would you do differently?"
